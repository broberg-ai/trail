# F207.4 — a key that stops calling must say so

**Card:** trail-F207.4 · **Parent:** F207 (no credential reaches git) · 3 SP · high

> Raised by **buddy**, 2 September 2026, immediately after we jointly closed a
> three-month outage. Their question is worth more than the outage:
>
> *"har I noget i JERES ende der ville opdage at en aktiv integration holdt op
> med at kalde?"*

## The answer is no, and the shape of the no is the point

Measured before replying:

| | |
|---|---|
| `control_api_keys.last_used_at` | exists |
| `proxy.ts:210` | stamps it on **every** call |
| `settings-account.tsx:592` | renders it ("Never used" when null) |
| any alarm / cron / upmetrics on it | **none** |

buddy's two `/local-ingest` probe jobs ran **every 120 seconds from 4 June** with
a revoked key, got 401 every time, and dispatched nothing. That silence was
written into *our* database daily — as a date that stopped moving — on a page
nobody opened.

The data was correct, current, and on screen for three months.

## Why this is the worst of the three

The same failure shape hit the fleet three times in two days:

| Where | The signal | Was anything broken? |
|---|---|---|
| buddy's dispatcher | 401 read as "0 pending" | yes — their F310 |
| our lint-drain | `rejected` hard-zeroed without `apply` | yes — our F212.4 |
| **this one** | a date that stopped moving | **no** |

The other two had a defect to fix. This one has nothing broken at all. We had
the answer and never asked the question.

## The distinction that is the whole design

**"Used to call and stopped" and "never called" are different events.**

A key minted for a script someone abandoned is noise. A key that called 720
times a day and then went quiet is an outage. Alarm on both identically and the
alarm becomes the thing people mute — which is how we would rebuild the blind
instrument in the very act of removing it.

That is not a hypothetical risk here; it is the exact mistake this card is a
reaction to. An instrument that cannot separate two states is not an
instrument, whichever two states they are.

## The strongest case needs no waiting period

A **revoked** key that is still being **presented** is detectable instantly, and
only we can see it:

- we set `revoked_at` — so we know the credential is dead;
- the 401 passes through our proxy — so we know something is still using it.

Nobody else in the fleet holds both halves. buddy's F310 fixes the consumer end
of the pipe (stop reading 401 as zero); this fixes the issuer end (tell them the
credential is dead). Both ends need it — a revoked key that does not shout at
the issuer costs the consumer again next time, whatever they build.

## Design sketch

A daily job over `control_api_keys`, reporting through **upmetrics** like every
other signal in this repo — not a bespoke notification path, which would just be
a second thing to forget to look at.

```
revoked AND presented recently   →  event now, no waiting window
has history, quiet > N days      →  event: "stopped calling"
never used, older than N days    →  event: "never used"   (different, lower)
never used, minted today         →  nothing
calling normally                 →  nothing
```

## Non-goals

- **Not** auto-revoking or auto-disabling anything. The remedy for a quiet key is
  a human looking at it; an automated revoke would turn a monitoring gap into an
  outage of its own.
- Not an error-rate alarm. The three months produced **no errors on our side** —
  the calls simply were not there, and a rate-based alarm has nothing to rate.
- Not a general uptime monitor. This is about credentials we issued.

## Verification

The regression test is the real incident, not a hypothetical: seed a key whose
call history ends 90 days ago and assert the detector would have raised it.

And the negative controls are load-bearing, as always — a detector that fires on
a healthy fleet is worse than no detector, because the first false alarm is what
teaches everyone to ignore the true one.
